"Architecture as Data" is
a concept that reimagines the built environment, software structures, and organisational workflows not as static physical or logical entities, but as dynamic, quantifiable, and programmable information.

Depending on the field, it refers to:
1. The Built Environment (Physical Architecture)
   In modern urbanism and design, this perspective views buildings and cities as "data-production machines".

   Sensory Integration: Architects shift from designing for human needs to orchestrating data flows by embedding sensors to monitor energy use, occupancy, and environmental impact.
   Digital Preservation: If architecture is data, the physical building is just one "instance." Digital renderings, historical metadata, and 3D models allow projects to leave a lasting cultural "imprint" even if the physical structure is never built or eventually destroyed.
   Environmental Impact: Architects act as data scientists, using quantitative analysis to measure waste (e.g., metric tons of paper per square meter) and global energy consumption (architecture accounts for ~55% of global electricity).

2. Software & Systems (Data Architecture)
   In IT, this refers to the structural foundation that organizes and activates information to drive business value.

   Model-Based Design: Documentation of software architecture is treated as a dataset itself. By modelling systems in data, organizations create a "single source of truth" for services and data stores, allowing diagrams to be re-rendered automatically whenever the underlying model changes.
   Executable Architecture: "Spec-driven development" turns architectural intent into validated, executable code, reducing the gap between high-level design and actual implementation.
   Modern Patterns: This includes Data Mesh (decentralising ownership to domains) and Data Fabric (creating a unified management layer across various environments).

3. Emerging Trends

   Near-Data Processing (NDP): Moving computation closer to where data is stored to solve bottlenecks in AI and machine learning.
   Medallion Architecture: A data management framework that organises information into layers (Bronze, Silver, Gold) based on its level of refinement and processing.
   Dataism: A philosophical shift where the goal of design is to serve algorithms and data-driven "happiness metrics," as seen in projects like Sidewalk Labs' Quayside.
