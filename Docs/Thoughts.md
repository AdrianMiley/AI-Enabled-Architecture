I was having a conversation this morning with one of my kids @Daniel Miley about AI in the workplace.

The thing that worried him was the number of people that just blindly follow whatever the AI tells them to do. These are of course the same people that prior to AI just blindly follow what other people (mostly their managers) told them to do.

So one of the points we discussed was Small Language Models (SLM) vs. Large Language Models (LLM) because I firmly believe that SLM's is the direction AI will go in eventually. That bit reminded me of the days of Knowledge Based Systems (KBS) - I was around for when these first started to emerge in the 1990's and they had a lot of early promise but died a quick death because it was very, very difficult for Subject Matter Experts (SME) to write the rules to configure a KBS with sufficient knowledge to be useful other than as first-level support.

AI doesn't have those limitation - it doesn't require facts to be exactly stated in some programmer-like language

However, what it does need is to be presented with a Knowledgebase from which to build the SML and that Knowledgebase needs to be populated and kept up to date. That requires an SME who can both write down what the rules are and, more importantly, explain why those are the rules and why other alternatives may look attractive but are less desirable.

So, the point being taht knowlege is always going to be in demand especially knowlege that evolves with the times and the circumstances. 

AI is not going to completely replace that and if Small Language Models are the way it goes then having hands on knowedge of some subject area be it Information Technology, Medicine, Logistics or whatever is going to be a very valuable skill to have in the future.
The ability to explain and teach is going to be even more important because the SLM will need to be trained and that training is going to require a lot of explanation and teaching to get it right.
