#   Suitable Approaches

When I first got into Information Technology, way back in the mists of time (the 1980s), most application development was focussed on Structured Systems Analysis and Design and, I hate to say it, Big Up-Front Design building Monolithic Applications.

However, things evolve and over the years I've picked up various ideas and concepts cherry-picked from the various Application Development Methodologies that regularly appear and believe are important to include in an Architecture Framework. 
Here are some of the key methodologies (in no particular order) that contain techniques that I find very useful when designing and building software systems:

| Term                         | Abbreviation | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
|------------------------------|--------------|-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Domain Driven Design         | DDD          | An approach to software development that emphasizes the importance of understanding and modeling the core business domain. DDD encourages collaboration between domain experts and developers to create a shared language and a rich model that accurately reflects the business processes and rules. This model is then used to guide the design and implementation of software systems, ensuring that they align closely with business needs.                                                                                                                 |
| C4                           | C4           | A hierarchical approach to visualizing software architecture, consisting of four levels of abstraction: Context, Container, Component, and Code. The C4 Model provides a clear and structured way to communicate the architecture of a software system to different stakeholders, from high-level overviews to detailed technical designs.                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| Model Driven Engineering     | MDE          | A software development approach that focuses on creating and exploiting domain models as primary artifacts in the development process. MDE emphasizes the use of models to generate code, documentation, and other artifacts, allowing for higher levels of abstraction and automation. This approach aims to improve productivity, maintainability, and alignment with business requirements by reducing manual coding and promoting model consistency.                                                                                                        |
| Consumer Driven Contracts    | CDC          | A design approach that focuses on defining and managing the interactions between services or components based on the needs and expectations of the consumers (clients) of those services. CDC emphasizes the importance of clear and explicit contracts that specify the behavior, data formats, and communication protocols required by consumers, allowing for better decoupling, flexibility, and evolution of services over time.                                                                                                                           |
| Test Driven Development      | TDD          | A software development process that relies on the repetition of a very short development cycle: first, the developer writes an (initially failing) automated test case that defines a desired improvement or new function; then produces the minimum amount of code to pass that test; and finally refactors the new code to acceptable standards. TDD encourages simple designs and inspires confidence in the codebase by ensuring that all code is tested and that tests are written before the code itself.                                                 |
| Behaviour Driven Development | BDD          | An extension of Test Driven Development that emphasizes collaboration between developers, QA, and non-technical or business participants in a software project. BDD focuses on defining the behavior of software in terms of user stories and scenarios, using a common language that all stakeholders can understand. This approach helps to ensure that the software meets the needs of its users and that everyone involved has a clear understanding of the requirements and expected outcomes.                                                             |
| Joint Application Design     | JAD          | A methodology that involves the client or end user in the design and development of an application through a series of collaborative workshops. JAD sessions bring together stakeholders, developers, and designers to discuss requirements, design options, and implementation strategies in a structured and facilitated environment. This approach helps to ensure that the final product meets the needs of its users and that all parties have a shared understanding of the project goals and requirements.                                               |
| Outcome Based Architecture   | OBA          | An architectural approach that focuses on defining and designing software systems based on the desired outcomes or business goals they are intended to achieve. OBA emphasizes the importance of understanding the value that a system will provide to its users and stakeholders, and designing the architecture in a way that maximizes that value. This approach encourages architects to think about the long-term impact of their design decisions and to prioritize features and capabilities that directly contribute to achieving the desired outcomes. |
| Fortress Architecture        | FA           | A security-focused architectural approach that emphasizes the design and implementation of software systems with robust security measures to protect against threats and vulnerabilities. Fortress Architecture involves incorporating security principles and practices throughout the software development lifecycle, including secure coding practices, threat modeling, and the use of security frameworks and tools. This approach aims to create resilient systems that can withstand attacks and protect sensitive data.                                 |

All of these approaches are very much complementary and can be used together when producing and maintaining an architecture but not all of them are necessarily desirable for any given project. 
The choice of which approach to use will depend on the specific needs and constraints of the project, as well as the preferences and expertise of the engineering team.

Within those approaches there are also a number of core artefacts that are commonly used to capture the key information about the system being designed and built. 
These include:

| Artefact                   | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
|----------------------------|------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Business Information Model | A high-level conceptual model that defines the key business entities, their attributes, and relationships within a specific domain or organization. The BIM serves as a bridge between business stakeholders and technical teams, providing a common understanding of the data requirements and structure needed to support business processes and objectives.                                                                                                                                 |
| Interface Definitions      | Defined points of interaction between different components or systems, specifying the methods, data formats, and protocols for communication. Interfaces are crucial for ensuring that different parts of a software system can work together effectively, allowing for modularity, flexibility, and scalability in the design and implementation of software solutions.                                                                                                                       |
| Workflow Definitions       | A sequence of tasks or activities that are performed to achieve a specific outcome or complete a business process. Workflows define the flow of information and the interactions between different components or systems, helping to ensure that processes are executed efficiently and consistently. They can be represented visually using flowcharts or diagrams, and are often used in conjunction with other architectural artifacts to capture the dynamic aspects of a software system. |
| State Machines             | A behavioural model that describes a system in terms of its states that it can be in and the transitions between those states. State machines are used to model the dynamic behavior of software systems, capturing how they respond to different inputs and events over time. State machines are particularly useful for modeling complex systems with multiple states and interactions.                                                                                                      |
